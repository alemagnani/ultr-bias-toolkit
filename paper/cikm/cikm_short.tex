\begin{abstract}
Intervention harvesting~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} estimates the
position-bias ratio $p_k/p_{k'}$ between positions $k$ and $k'$ by
aggregating, across many $(\mathrm{query},\mathrm{doc})$ cells indexed
by $i$, the per-cell click-rate ratio at the two positions. Let
$N_k^i$ denote the impression count of cell $i$ at position $k$. On
real click logs these counts are extremely heavy-tailed: on the full
KDD Cup 2012 Track 2 log, $60\%$ of interventional $(q,\mathrm{ad})$
pairs have $\le 5$ total impressions while $0.1\%$ of pairs hold
$42\%$ of all impressions. The way cells are weighted is therefore
decisive. The intervention-harvesting estimator of Agarwal et al.\
uses \emph{uniform} per-cell weighting; we propose two new count-only
alternatives---a simple \emph{min-count} weight
$\omega_i=\min(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$ and a \emph{harmonic} weight
$\omega_i=N_k^iN_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$.
\emph{Why harmonic?} Under the Position-Based Model (PBM), the
per-impression click variance is $p_k r_i(1-p_k r_i)/N_k^i$, which
depends on the unknown per-cell relevance $r_i$. Replacing the
$r_i$-dependent factor with a constant yields a \emph{count-only
surrogate} of the Bernoulli variance that is exact when the per-cell
relevance $r_i$ is constant across cells, and a controlled
approximation otherwise. Among count-only weights, harmonic is the inverse-variance
optimum on this surrogate, and a Cauchy--Schwarz inequality guarantees
it reduces the surrogate for any impression counts. The full
inverse-variance optimum would require knowing $r_i$ per cell and is
infeasible on heavy-tailed data. We assess finite-sample efficiency at
fixed weights via the closed-form delta-method asymptotic variance of
the weighted ratio estimator~\cite{owen2013mc,vdv1998asymptotic}. On
the full KDD log, harmonic's asymptotic relative efficiency over
uniform weighting is $4.5{\times}$ and remains robust to weight
capping. Under PBM mis-specification, different weight schemes target
different weighted averages of cell-specific ratios---an estimand
caveat we make explicit. Synthetic and Yahoo-LTR semi-synthetic
experiments with ground truth corroborate the efficiency prediction
(up to $54\%$ variance reduction at high imbalance); min and harmonic
are empirically comparable, and we recommend harmonic for its
unconditional theoretical guarantee.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Position bias systematically corrupts click signals used for
learning-to-rank~\cite{joachims2017unbiased}; intervention
harvesting~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} estimates position-bias ratios
$R_{k,k'}=p_k/p_{k'}$ from naturally occurring ranking variations without
explicit randomisation. On production click logs, the per-cell counts that
feed this estimator are extraordinarily heavy-tailed. On the full
KDD Cup 2012 Track 2 release (235M impressions; 4.6M interventional pairs
at adjacent positions $(1,2)$), \textbf{60.7\% of pairs have
$N_1+N_2\le 5$ while 0.1\% of pairs carry 42\% of all
impressions}~\cite{kddcup2012}. Public benchmarks can hide this shape: the released
Baidu-ULTR~\cite{zou2022large} aggregation has $94\%$ of $(q,d,\mathrm{pos})$
cells with $N=1$ and $78\%$ of adjacent-pair impression ratios within
$[0.5,2]$, which we attribute to the per-cell deduplication used in the
public release rather than to the underlying production logs (the
unaggregated KDD release, by contrast, preserves the heavy-tailed
production distribution).

Equal-weighting heteroscedastic estimators is statistically suboptimal
by classical inverse-variance theory~\cite{borenstein2009meta}, a result
the meta-analysis literature has championed for decades. The intervention-harvesting estimator
of~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} uses uniform per-cell weighting (each
cell's click rate enters the sum with weight $1$). \textbf{We propose
two new count-aware alternatives}: the \emph{min-count} weight
$\omega_i=\min(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$ and the \emph{harmonic} weight
$\omega_i=N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$. Empirically, min and
harmonic perform comparably on KDD (Section~\ref{sec:kdd}); the
harmonic proposal is preferable because of its stronger theoretical
foundation: it is the inverse-variance optimum of a relevance-agnostic
surrogate (Theorem~\ref{thm:ivw}) and the only one of the two with an
unconditional variance-reduction guarantee against uniform
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cs}). Existing variance-reduction arguments on
heavy-tailed real logs have relied on \emph{bootstrap} resampling,
which we show can be misleading for inverse-variance-style weights on
singleton-dominated data (Section~\ref{sec:plugin}).

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} We show that the harmonic weight is the inverse-variance
optimum on a \emph{relevance-agnostic count-only surrogate} of the
per-cell variance under PBM (Theorem~\ref{thm:ivw}), and prove a
Cauchy--Schwarz inequality showing it reduces this surrogate for any
impression counts (Theorem~\ref{thm:cs}). The exact inverse-variance
oracle for the more general asymmetric surrogate is also characterised,
and harmonic is its parameter-free special case (Theorem~\ref{thm:asym}).
\textbf{(2)} We adopt the textbook closed-form asymptotic variance of
the weighted ratio estimator as a finite-sample diagnostic that is
properly aligned with conditional-on-counts efficiency on heavy-tailed
data, where naive pair-bootstrap is misleading.
\textbf{(3)} On the full KDD log, harmonic delivers an asymptotic
relative efficiency of $4.53$ over uniform on this surrogate -- meaning
uniform weighting would need roughly $4.5{\times}$ the data to match
harmonic's precision -- with the plug-in agreeing within $1$--$2\%$ of
empirical click-resample standard error and remaining robust to weight
capping. A Cochran-$Q$ heterogeneity test ($Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$) confirms
substantial PBM mis-specification, under which the choice of weight is
a precision/estimand trade-off rather than a search for a single
``true'' position-bias ratio. Synthetic and Yahoo-LTR experiments with
ground truth corroborate.

\section{Estimator and Theory}
\label{sec:method}

Throughout, $q$ denotes a query, $d$ a document or ad shown for that
query, and $k$ a result position; $N_k^i$ and $C_k^i$ are the number
of impressions and clicks the $i$-th $(q,d)$ pair received at position
$k$. The Position-Based Model assumes $P(\mathrm{click}\mid q,d,k)
=p_k\,r_{q,d}$, factorising click probability into a position-only
examination probability $p_k$ and a relevance term $r_{q,d}\in[0,1]$.
Given the set of $(q,d)$ pairs that appeared at both positions $k$ and
$k'$, the ratio $p_k/p_{k'}$ is estimated
as~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}
\begin{equation}
\hat R_{k,k'}=\frac{A}{B}=\frac{\sum_i \omega_i\, \hat\theta_k^i}
                              {\sum_i \omega_i\, \hat\theta_{k'}^i},
\quad \hat\theta_k^i=\tfrac{C_k^i}{N_k^i}.
\label{eq:Rhat}
\end{equation}
$\hat R_{k,k'}$ is the standard \emph{self-normalised weighted ratio}
estimator: a sum of weighted per-cell click rates divided by another
such sum. We say a weight scheme is \emph{ratio-unbiased} when, under
PBM, the ratio of the conditional expectations of the numerator and
denominator equals $p_k/p_{k'}$ -- note this is not the same as
$\mathbb E[\hat R]=p_k/p_{k'}$, because expectation does not commute
with ratios; the finite-sample bias of $\hat R$ is $O(1/N)$ under
standard regularity. Under PBM, every count-only weight
$\omega_i=\omega(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$ satisfies this property, and $\hat R$
is consistent for $p_k/p_{k'}$~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional delta-method variance]
\label{prop:delta}
By the delta method~\cite{vdv1998asymptotic},
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat R\mid\mathbf N)\approx R^2[\alpha
V_k(\omega)+\beta V_{k'}(\omega)]$ with
$V_r(\omega)=\sum_i\omega_i^2/N_r^i/(\sum_i\omega_i)^2$,
$\alpha=(1-p_k)/p_k$, $\beta=(1-p_{k'})/p_{k'}$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Relevance-agnostic count-only IVW.}
The exact Bernoulli variance is
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta_k^i)=p_kr_i(1-p_kr_i)/N_k^i$, which depends on
the cell relevance $r_i$. Plug-in inverse-variance weights with
$\hat r_i$ estimated per cell define the
\emph{$\theta$-aware oracle} of Theorem~\ref{thm:asym}; in
heavy-tailed click logs $\hat r_i$ is itself extremely noisy on the
$\sim\!60\%$ singleton mass, making this oracle infeasible in practice
(Table~\ref{tab:are_full}: ARE $= 0.002$). We therefore work with a
\emph{relevance-agnostic surrogate} that drops the $r_i$-dependent
factor from the cell-level variance; this surrogate is exact when the
per-cell relevance $r_i$ is constant across cells, and a controlled
approximation otherwise. The inverse-variance
optimum~\cite{borenstein2009meta} on this surrogate, restricted to
count-only weights, is the harmonic mean of the two cell counts. We make this
precise:

\begin{theorem}[Count-only IVW on the surrogate]
\label{thm:ivw}
Among all non-negative weights $\omega_i=\omega(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$, harmonic
weighting minimises the count-only delta-method variance surrogate
$V_k(\omega)+V_{k'}(\omega)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Surrogate guarantee, unconditional in counts]
\label{thm:cs}
For any impression counts $\{N_k^i,N_{k'}^i\}_{i=1}^n$,
$\;V_k(\omega^h)+V_{k'}(\omega^h)\le V_k(\mathbf 1)+V_{k'}(\mathbf 1)$.
The guarantee is unconditional in the counts but conditional on
$V_k+V_{k'}$ as the objective; it implies improved
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat R\mid\mathbf N)$ via
Proposition~\ref{prop:delta} when $\alpha\approx\beta$. For the
asymmetric $\alpha V_k+\beta V_{k'}$ surrogate the bound is empirical
only, but we verify on full KDD that harmonic reduces it by essentially
the same factor as the symmetric one (Section~\ref{sec:kdd}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Substituting $\omega_i^h=N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$ collapses the LHS to
$1/W$ with $W=\sum_i\omega_i^h$. The RHS is
$(1/n^2)\sum_i 1/\omega_i^h$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$n^2\le W\sum_i 1/\omega_i^h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact asymmetric oracle]
\label{thm:asym}
$\alpha V_k(\omega)+\beta V_{k'}(\omega)\ge(\sum_i 1/s_i)^{-1}$ with
$s_i=\alpha/N_k^i+\beta/N_{k'}^i$, attained at
$\omega_i^\star\propto N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(\alpha N_{k'}^i+\beta N_k^i)$.
Harmonic is the $\alpha=\beta$ special case.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cs} is unconditional in the counts -- a property
that distinguishes harmonic from the min-count variant, which has no
such guarantee. Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} provides the oracle
benchmark; in synthetic experiments substituting the true $\alpha,\beta$ into
$\omega^\star$ yields variance within $\pm 2\%$ of harmonic. Complete
proofs and additional sensitivity analyses are provided in the
supplementary material.

\paragraph{Limits of the symmetric guarantee.}
The unconditional bound in Theorem~\ref{thm:cs} concerns
$V_k+V_{k'}$. The asymmetric analogue
$\alpha V_k(\omega^h)+\beta V_{k'}(\omega^h) \le \alpha V_k(\mathbf 1)+\beta V_{k'}(\mathbf 1)$
does \emph{not} hold unconditionally: a closed-form $n=2$ counterexample
with $(N_k^1,N_{k'}^1)=(1,100)$, $(N_k^2,N_{k'}^2)=(100,1)$ and
$(\alpha,\beta)=(0.01,1)$ has harmonic strictly worse than uniform on the
asymmetric surrogate. The configurations under which this fails are
degenerate (each cell informative at exactly one position); we have not
encountered them in any realistic count distribution we tested. The
$\omega^\star$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} remains the exact oracle for
all $(\alpha,\beta)$.

\section{Plug-in Asymptotic Variance as Diagnostic}
\label{sec:plugin}

\paragraph{$\widetilde V$ in closed form.}
With $A=\sum_i\omega_i\theta_k^i$, $B=\sum_i\omega_i\theta_{k'}^i$, and
clicks at $k$ and $k'$ conditionally independent given counts, the delta
method gives
\begin{equation}
\widetilde V(\omega)=\frac{\sum_i\omega_i^2\,\theta_k^i(1-\theta_k^i)/N_k^i}{A^2}
+\frac{\sum_i\omega_i^2\,\theta_{k'}^i(1-\theta_{k'}^i)/N_{k'}^i}{B^2},
\label{eq:Vtilde}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{SE}(\hat R)=|\hat R|\sqrt{\widetilde V(\omega)}$. Plugging in
$\hat\theta_i$ (with the shrinkage estimator
$\hat\theta_i=(C_i+\bar\theta_k)/(N_i+1)$, $\bar\theta_k$ the marginal
position-$k$ CTR) yields the textbook
self-normalised IS variance estimator~\cite{owen2013mc,kong1992ess,swaminathan2015snips}.

\paragraph{ARE.} For schemes $a,b$,
$\mathrm{ARE}(a,b)=\widetilde V(\omega_b)/\widetilde V(\omega_a)$:
scheme $b$ needs $\mathrm{ARE}(a,b)$ times the data of $a$ for equal
precision. $\widetilde V$ scales as $1/\mathrm{ESS}(\omega)$, with
Kong's effective sample size~\cite{kong1992ess}
$\mathrm{ESS}(\omega)=(\sum_i\omega_i)^2/\sum_i\omega_i^2$.

\paragraph{Why pair-bootstrap is misaligned here.}
A naive bootstrap that resamples $(q,d)$ pairs estimates a different
quantity than the conditional-on-counts efficiency we care about, for three
reasons. \emph{(i)} Averaging over $\sim 60\%$ near-singleton pairs under
uniform produces a smooth resampling distribution whose tightness reflects
averaging-of-noise rather than estimator precision. \emph{(ii)} Pair
resampling perturbs the high-information cells that drive any
inverse-variance estimator, so concentrated weight schemes are
systematically penalised by the resampling design, not by their statistical
behaviour. \emph{(iii)} Pair-bootstrap entangles estimator efficiency with
model fit; $\widetilde V$ separates them (Section~\ref{sec:cochran}).
The appropriate empirical baseline against which to validate the plug-in
is the click-resample (parametric Bernoulli bootstrap conditional on
counts), which we adopt for calibration.

\paragraph{Calibration and stability at scale.}
Table~\ref{tab:scaling} reports plug-in SE versus empirical
click-resample SE across KDD sample fractions, together with
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})$ at each scale. Because
both $\widetilde V$ and click-resample SE share the conditional
Bernoulli + cross-cell-independence assumption, their agreement is
primarily an implementation-correctness check; a stronger probe of the
independence assumption would be a query-cluster bootstrap, which is
sensitive to Cochran-$Q$-style heterogeneity. We report pair-bootstrap
numbers in supplementary material. The ARE \emph{grows} with sample
size ($1.66 \to 4.53$) because the heavy-tailed structure of the count
distribution becomes more pronounced as more data is included rather
than averaged out.

\paragraph{Headline (Table~\ref{tab:are_full}).}
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})=4.53$:
\textbf{uniform requires $\sim 4.5\times$ the impressions of harmonic
for matching precision} on the relevance-agnostic surrogate. The
min-count proposal achieves $4.41$ on the same surrogate ($-2.7\%$
relative): harmonic and min are essentially tied empirically on KDD,
and we recommend harmonic because it carries the unconditional
guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:cs}) and the parameter-free derivation,
not because of a large empirical margin. The count-only oracle
($4.57$) is the ceiling within the surrogate. The $\theta$-aware
oracle, which would be optimal under classical inverse-variance theory
with known $r_i$, plugs in $\hat r_i$
that is too noisy on the singleton-dominated tail and collapses to
$\mathrm{ARE}=0.002$: a substantive finding rather than a side
note---it shows that count-only weighting is robust precisely where
plug-in IVW fails.

\paragraph{Asymmetric surrogate (empirical).}
With $\hat p_1=0.0501$, $\hat p_2=0.0256$ estimated from the marginal
CTRs, $\alpha=18.97$, $\beta=38.08$, $\alpha/\beta\approx 0.50$.
Harmonic reduces $\alpha V_k+\beta V_{k'}$ by a factor of $4.75$ versus
uniform. Both this number and the symmetric reduction of $4.84$ are
deterministic functionals of the observed counts---the gap reflects the
intrinsic asymmetry of $(\alpha,\beta)$, not Monte-Carlo noise---and the
two are within $2\%$ of each other. The closed-form asymmetric
counterexample of Section~\ref{sec:method} requires strongly
\emph{anti-correlated} $(N_k,N_{k'})$ across cells (each cell informative
at exactly one position); on KDD the per-pair counts are positively
correlated, as items popular at position $1$ are typically also popular
at position $2$, and we conjecture this is generic for production logs.

\paragraph{Stratified ARE (Table~\ref{tab:strat}).}
Schemes tie on noise singletons (where there is nothing to estimate) and
harmonic dominates uniform by $5\times$ on the high-information stratum.
The gain comes from \emph{not} treating singletons as equal to
million-impression cells.

\paragraph{Weight-cap robustness.}
The harmonic ESS is $1{,}849$ out of $4.61$M pairs: concentration on a
small subset of high-count cells is what produces the surrogate
$\widetilde V$ reduction, and the trade-off is sensitivity to those
cells, which we probe directly by capping. Capping $\omega_i^h$ at
the $99.99$th percentile (threshold $2{,}079$; $462$ pairs capped) lifts
ESS to $5.2{\times}10^4$ and still gives
$\mathrm{ARE}=4.12$; at the $99$th percentile, $\mathrm{ARE}=2.83$ (ESS
$7.6{\times}10^5$); even at the $90$th, $\mathrm{ARE}=1.86$. Harmonic
beats uniform robustly across capping levels.

\paragraph{Estimand under PBM mis-specification.}
\label{par:estimand}
PBM is a known approximation; on real logs different weight schemes
estimate different functionals of cell-specific ratios. Specifically, the
population limit of $\hat R$ under any weight $\omega$ is
\[
R_\omega \;=\; \frac{\sum_i \omega_i\, \theta_k^i}{\sum_i \omega_i\, \theta_{k'}^i},
\]
which equals $p_k/p_{k'}$ only when $\theta_k^i/\theta_{k'}^i$ is the same
constant across cells---i.e.\ when PBM holds exactly. Under mis-specification
each scheme thus targets a different weighted average of the cell-specific
ratios $\rho_i=\theta_k^i/\theta_{k'}^i$ (uniform $\to 1.68$, harmonic
$\to 1.71$, $\theta$-aware oracle $\to 2.34$ on KDD). The plug-in
$\widetilde V$ comparison is a precision statement at fixed
$\omega$---a model-based efficiency comparison---and does \emph{not} claim
that one $R_\omega$ is the ``correct'' position-bias ratio. Choosing
$\omega$ thus involves a precision/estimand trade-off; harmonic's
attractive property is that, among count-only schemes, it concentrates on
high-information cells (where $\rho_i$ is estimated most precisely)
without depending on noisy plug-in $\hat\theta_i$.

\paragraph{Quantifying PBM mis-specification (Cochran's $Q$).}
\label{sec:cochran}
PBM is a known approximation, not a literal model -- prior
work~\cite{yue2010beyond,chuklin2015clickmodels} has long
established that real logs violate it. The relevant question is how
\emph{large} the deviation is. Cochran's $Q$ on the $16{,}132$
high-information pairs ($\ge 5$ clicks at each of positions $1$ and $2$)
gives $Q=6.17{\times}10^4$, $\mathrm{df}=16{,}131$, $Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$:
per-cell odds-ratio variability is $\sim 3.8\times$ what binomial sampling
alone would produce, well outside Poisson dispersion. This magnitude is
the quantitative driver of the estimand drift discussed in
Paragraph~\ref{par:estimand}; plug-in $\widetilde V$ keeps the efficiency
comparison well-posed by working at fixed $\omega$.

\section{Synthetic and Yahoo-LTR Validation}
\label{sec:synth}

To validate the ARE prediction in settings with ground truth, we run two
controlled experiments.

\paragraph{Anchor-item imbalance sweep (synthetic).}
Following~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}: $M{=}10$ positions, $p_k=k^{-\eta}$
with $\eta=1$, AdjacentChain estimator. A fraction $s$ of documents are
``anchors'' with heavy traffic at one position
($N\sim\mathrm{Unif}(50r,200r)$) and light traffic at the other
($N\sim\mathrm{Unif}(1,51)$); the remaining $1-s$ are balanced
($N\sim\mathrm{Unif}(25,75)$ at both positions); $s\in\{0.5,...,0.95\}$,
$r\in\{1,...,100\}$, 500 trials per cell. At $s=0.80$, $r=100$ harmonic
reduces variance by $43.6\%$ vs.\ uniform and $5\%$ vs.\ min; reduction
grows to $\sim 54\%$ at $s=0.90,r=50$. Across the full $(s,r)$ grid the
data-equivalence factor
$\mathrm{DEF}(s,r)=b_{\mathrm{uni}}(s,r)/b_{\mathrm{harm}}(s,r)$ -- the
ratio of base impressions per document at which each scheme first hits a
fixed MSE threshold -- has median $1.31$, mean $1.30$, max $1.94$. Highly
imbalanced cells achieve up to $2\times$ data savings; near-balanced
cells ($r=1$) saturate at $\mathrm{DEF}\approx 1$.

\paragraph{Bias comparability under PBM violations.}
We additionally simulate three explicit PBM violation models -- trust
bias, cascade, and position-dependent
relevance~\cite{craswell2008experimental,agarwal2019estimating} -- at
imbalance ratio $50{:}1$, split $80/20$, $5{,}000$ trials. Across all
three models the count-only schemes (uniform, min, harmonic) exhibit
\emph{comparable} bias$^2$, so harmonic's lower variance translates
directly into lower MSE (Table~\ref{tab:pbm_viol}). Under cascade, where
history-dependent examination introduces a substantial structural mismatch
($\mathrm{bias}^2\approx 0.011$ for every ratio-unbiased scheme), the
variance edge is preserved but the practical MSE gain shrinks because
bias dominates.

\paragraph{Yahoo-LTR semi-synthetic.}
Same simulator with graded relevance from the Yahoo Learning to Rank
Challenge Set~1 test split (6,936 queries; relevance grades $g\in\{0,...,4\}$
mapped to $r(q,d)=(2^g-1)/15$ following NDCG; click model
$P(C=1\mid q,d,k)=p_k\,r(q,d)$; 200 trials). Harmonic reduces variance by
$\sim 40\%$ vs.\ uniform at imbalance $r=50$ (Var
$1.5\!\times\!10^{-4}\!\to\!8.7\!\times\!10^{-5}$). With $200$ trials,
a delta-method approximation places the $95\%$ CI on the
variance-ratio at $[0.49, 0.71]$, so the gain is well separated from
$1.0$ but not tightly pinned. Aggressive clipping trades variance for
bias and yields $\sim\!458\times$ higher MSE.

setting) shows harmonic's MSE reduction growing from $6.3\%$ to
$17.2\%$ as adjacent-pair impression imbalance rises---qualitative
direction only; full sweep deferred to the regular-paper version.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Intervention harvesting was introduced
by~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}; \cite{wang2018position} jointly
estimates relevance and propensities via EM. Self-normalised weighted
ratio estimators are standard in importance sampling and survey
statistics~\cite{owen2013mc,kong1992ess,hesterberg1995};
inverse-variance weighting is canonical in
meta-analysis~\cite{borenstein2009meta}.
The closest off-policy analogue is
SNIPS~\cite{swaminathan2015snips,bottou2013counterfactual}. PBM
violations on real logs are documented
in~\cite{yue2010beyond,chuklin2015clickmodels}.

\paragraph{Why count-only PBM weighting is still worth studying.}
Given that PBM is approximate, EM-style joint estimation of relevance
and propensities~\cite{wang2018position} is a natural alternative
with its own bias--variance trade-offs (initialisation sensitivity,
model choice). The two approaches are complementary: a
relevance-agnostic weight is the right input to an EM M-step that
re-uses count statistics. Empirical EM comparison is left to the
regular-paper version.

\section{Conclusion}

Harmonic weighting is the inverse-variance optimum on a
\emph{relevance-agnostic count-only surrogate} of the PBM delta-method
variance (Theorems~\ref{thm:ivw}--\ref{thm:asym}). The actual
inverse-variance solution ($\theta$-aware oracle) is plug-in infeasible
on singleton-dominated data ($\mathrm{ARE}=0.002$). The plug-in
$\widetilde V$ is the natural diagnostic at fixed $\omega$; on full KDD
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})=4.53$ on the surrogate,
with our two proposals tied empirically (harmonic $4.53$, min $4.41$;
harmonic is preferred because it carries the unconditional guarantee
of Theorem~\ref{thm:cs}). Under
known PBM mis-specification (Cochran-$Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$) different
schemes target different weighted averages of cell-specific ratios, so
choosing $\omega$ is a precision/estimand trade-off. Future work:
shrinkage $\theta$-aware oracle; EM comparison~\cite{wang2018position};
click models beyond PBM~\cite{yue2010beyond,chuklin2015clickmodels}.
