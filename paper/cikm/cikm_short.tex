\begin{abstract}
Intervention-harvesting estimators of position
bias~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} combine per-cell click-rate estimates
across many $(q,d,\mathrm{position})$ cells whose impression counts span
several orders of magnitude. Under the Position-Based Model (PBM), with
cell-level relevance treated as homoscedastic, the \emph{harmonic} weight
$\omega_i=N_k^iN_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$ is the count-only inverse-variance
choice---Aitken's classical generalised-least-squares
solution~\cite{aitken1934least} restricted to count-only weights---and an
unconditional Cauchy--Schwarz inequality shows it minimises the
delta-method variance \emph{surrogate} $V_k+V_{k'}$ for any impression
counts. We propose the plug-in delta-method asymptotic variance
$\widetilde V(\omega)$ of the self-normalised weighted ratio
estimator~\cite{owen2013mc,vdv1998asymptotic} as a finite-sample diagnostic
that is well aligned with the conditional-on-counts efficiency question, in
contrast to a naive pair-bootstrap. On the full KDD Cup 2012 Track 2
sponsored-search log (235M impressions, 4.6M interventional pairs), the
plug-in asymptotic relative efficiency of harmonic over uniform is $4.53$,
well-calibrated against click-resample standard error ($1$--$2\%$ at two
sample fractions), and robust to weight-capping (ARE $\ge 4.1$ at the
$99.99$th percentile). A Cochran-$Q$ diagnostic confirms substantial PBM mis-specification
($Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$), allowing efficiency comparisons to be interpreted
separately from model fit; under such mis-specification different weight
schemes target different weighted averages of cell-specific ratios---an
estimand caveat we discuss explicitly. Synthetic ($43\%$--$54\%$ MSE
reduction at high imbalance) and Yahoo-LTR semi-synthetic ($\sim 40\%$
variance reduction at $r=50$) experiments with ground truth corroborate the
efficiency prediction.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Position bias systematically corrupts click signals used for
learning-to-rank~\cite{joachims2017unbiased}; intervention
harvesting~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} estimates position-bias ratios
$R_{k,k'}=p_k/p_{k'}$ from naturally occurring ranking variations without
explicit randomisation. On production click logs, the per-cell counts that
feed this estimator are extraordinarily heavy-tailed. On the full
KDD Cup 2012 Track 2 release (235M impressions; 4.6M interventional pairs
at adjacent positions $(1,2)$), \textbf{60.7\% of pairs have
$N_1+N_2\le 5$ while 0.1\% of pairs carry 42\% of all
impressions}~\cite{kddcup2012}. Public benchmarks hide this shape: the released
Baidu-ULTR~\cite{zou2022large} aggregation has $94\%$ of $(q,d,\mathrm{pos})$
cells with $N=1$ and $78\%$ of adjacent-pair impression ratios within
$[0.5,2]$ \emph{because} the publication pipeline deduplicates impressions
per cell---an artefact of release engineering, not of the underlying
production logs. The unaggregated KDD release preserves the heavy-tailed
production distribution.

Equal-weighting heteroscedastic estimators is statistically suboptimal by a
century of classical theory: Aitken's generalised-least-squares
theorem~\cite{aitken1934least} mandates inverse-variance weights, a result
the meta-analysis literature~\cite{hedges1985meta,borenstein2009meta} has
championed for fifty years. Recent ULTR
work~\cite{agarwal2019estimating,wang2018position} introduced \emph{harmonic}
weighting $\omega_i=N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$ to reduce variance versus
uniform on heavy-tailed counts, but existing validation has relied on
\emph{bootstrap} resampling, which we show can be misleading for
inverse-variance-style weights on singleton-dominated heavy-tailed data
(Section~\ref{sec:plugin}).

\textbf{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} We identify harmonic as the count-only inverse-variance choice
under the standard PBM with homoscedastic relevance
(Theorem~\ref{thm:ivw}), prove an unconditional Cauchy--Schwarz reduction
of the delta-method variance \emph{surrogate} $V_k+V_{k'}$
(Theorem~\ref{thm:cs}, conditional on the surrogate objective and
unconditional in the counts), and characterise the exact asymmetric
surrogate oracle of which harmonic is the $\alpha=\beta$ special case
(Theorem~\ref{thm:asym}).
\textbf{(2)} We adopt the plug-in delta-method asymptotic variance
$\widetilde V(\omega)$ as a finite-sample efficiency diagnostic aligned
with the conditional-on-counts question, and use empirical click-resample
SE as its calibration baseline; we discuss why pair-bootstrap is misaligned
with this question on heavy-tailed data.
\textbf{(3)} On the full KDD log we measure
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})=4.53$, calibrated within
$1$--$2\%$ across two sample fractions, and robust to weight-capping at
the $99.99$th percentile (ARE $\ge 4.1$). We quantify PBM
mis-specification via Cochran's $Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$ and discuss the
estimand caveat that follows.
Synthetic and Yahoo-LTR experiments with ground truth corroborate.

\section{Estimator and Theory}
\label{sec:method}

Under PBM, $P(\mathrm{click}\mid q,d,k)=p_k\,r_{q,d}$. Given an interventional
set $I_{k,k'}$ of $(q,d)$ pairs that appeared at both positions $k$ and $k'$,
the position-bias ratio is estimated as~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}
\begin{equation}
\hat R_{k,k'}=\frac{A}{B}=\frac{\sum_i \omega_i\, \hat\theta_k^i}
                              {\sum_i \omega_i\, \hat\theta_{k'}^i},
\quad \hat\theta_k^i=\tfrac{C_k^i}{N_k^i}.
\label{eq:Rhat}
\end{equation}
This is a self-normalised weighted ratio estimator. We say a weight scheme
is \emph{ratio-unbiased} when the ratio of conditional expectations of
numerator and denominator equals $p_k/p_{k'}$,
i.e.\ $\mathbb E[A\mid\mathbf N]/\mathbb E[B\mid\mathbf N]=p_k/p_{k'}$
(\emph{not} $\mathbb E[\hat R]=p_k/p_{k'}$; the finite-sample bias of the
self-normalised ratio is $O(1/N)$ under standard regularity). Under PBM, the ratio of conditional expectations of numerator and
denominator equals $p_k/p_{k'}$ for any count-only weight
$\omega_i=\omega(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$, and the resulting self-normalised
ratio estimator is consistent for this target~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}.

\begin{proposition}[Conditional delta-method variance]
\label{prop:delta}
By the delta method~\cite{vdv1998asymptotic},
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat R\mid\mathbf N)\approx R^2[\alpha
V_k(\omega)+\beta V_{k'}(\omega)]$ with
$V_r(\omega)=\sum_i\omega_i^2/N_r^i/(\sum_i\omega_i)^2$,
$\alpha=(1-p_k)/p_k$, $\beta=(1-p_{k'})/p_{k'}$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Harmonic as count-only IVW.}
For Bernoulli observations,
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat\theta_k^i)=\theta_k^i(1-\theta_k^i)/N_k^i$. Under PBM,
$\theta_k^i=p_k r_i$, so the Bernoulli variance contains a cell-level
relevance $r_i$ that is generally heterogeneous. After suppressing this
cell-level relevance heterogeneity, the inverse-variance optimum of
Aitken~\cite{aitken1934least} restricted to count-only weights is the
harmonic mean of the two cell counts. We make this precise as a statement
about the variance \emph{surrogate} obtained by ignoring cell-level
relevance variation:

\begin{theorem}[Count-only IVW on the surrogate]
\label{thm:ivw}
Among all non-negative weights $\omega_i=\omega(N_k^i,N_{k'}^i)$, harmonic
weighting minimises the count-only delta-method variance surrogate
$V_k(\omega)+V_{k'}(\omega)$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Surrogate guarantee, unconditional in counts]
\label{thm:cs}
For any impression counts $\{N_k^i,N_{k'}^i\}_{i=1}^n$,
$\;V_k(\omega^h)+V_{k'}(\omega^h)\le V_k(\mathbf 1)+V_{k'}(\mathbf 1)$.
The guarantee is unconditional in the counts but conditional on
$V_k+V_{k'}$ as the objective; it implies improved
$\mathrm{Var}(\hat R\mid\mathbf N)$ under PBM when $\alpha\approx\beta$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:delta} below).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
Substituting $\omega_i^h=N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(N_k^i+N_{k'}^i)$ collapses the LHS to
$1/W$ with $W=\sum_i\omega_i^h$. The RHS is
$(1/n^2)\sum_i 1/\omega_i^h$. Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$n^2\le W\sum_i 1/\omega_i^h$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exact asymmetric oracle]
\label{thm:asym}
$\alpha V_k(\omega)+\beta V_{k'}(\omega)\ge(\sum_i 1/s_i)^{-1}$ with
$s_i=\alpha/N_k^i+\beta/N_{k'}^i$, attained at
$\omega_i^\star\propto N_k^i N_{k'}^i/(\alpha N_{k'}^i+\beta N_k^i)$.
Harmonic is the $\alpha=\beta$ special case.
\end{theorem}

Theorem~\ref{thm:cs} is unconditional in the counts -- a property that
distinguishes harmonic from the min-count heuristic of~\cite{agarwal2019estimating},
which has no such guarantee. Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} provides the oracle
benchmark; in synthetic experiments substituting the true $\alpha,\beta$ into
$\omega^\star$ yields variance within $\pm 2\%$ of harmonic. Complete
proofs and additional sensitivity analyses are provided in the
supplementary material.

\section{Plug-in Asymptotic Variance: the Right Instrument}
\label{sec:plugin}

\paragraph{$\widetilde V$ in closed form.}
With $A=\sum_i\omega_i\theta_k^i$, $B=\sum_i\omega_i\theta_{k'}^i$, and
clicks at $k$ and $k'$ conditionally independent given counts, the delta
method gives
\begin{equation}
\widetilde V(\omega)=\frac{\sum_i\omega_i^2\,\theta_k^i(1-\theta_k^i)/N_k^i}{A^2}
+\frac{\sum_i\omega_i^2\,\theta_{k'}^i(1-\theta_{k'}^i)/N_{k'}^i}{B^2},
\label{eq:Vtilde}
\end{equation}
$\mathrm{SE}(\hat R)=|\hat R|\sqrt{\widetilde V(\omega)}$. Plugging in
$\hat\theta_i$ (with light shrinkage toward the marginal) yields the textbook
self-normalised IS variance estimator~\cite{owen2013mc,kong1992ess,swaminathan2015snips}.

\paragraph{ARE.} For schemes $a,b$,
$\mathrm{ARE}(a,b)=\widetilde V(\omega_b)/\widetilde V(\omega_a)$:
scheme $b$ needs $\mathrm{ARE}(a,b)$ times the data of $a$ for equal
precision. $\widetilde V$ scales as $1/\mathrm{ESS}(\omega)$, with
Kong's effective sample size~\cite{kong1992ess}
$\mathrm{ESS}(\omega)=(\sum_i\omega_i)^2/\sum_i\omega_i^2$.

\paragraph{Why pair-bootstrap is misaligned here.}
A naive bootstrap that resamples $(q,d)$ pairs estimates a different
quantity than the conditional-on-counts efficiency we care about, for three
reasons. \emph{(i)} Averaging over $\sim 60\%$ near-singleton pairs under
uniform produces a smooth resampling distribution whose tightness reflects
averaging-of-noise rather than estimator precision. \emph{(ii)} Pair
resampling perturbs the high-information cells that drive any
inverse-variance estimator, so concentrated weight schemes are
systematically penalised by the resampling design, not by their statistical
behaviour. \emph{(iii)} Pair-bootstrap entangles estimator efficiency with
model fit; $\widetilde V$ separates them (Section~\ref{sec:cochran}).
The appropriate empirical baseline against which to validate the plug-in
is the click-resample (parametric Bernoulli bootstrap conditional on
counts), which we adopt for calibration.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
Empirical click-resample SE and the plug-in $\widetilde V$ agree closely
on KDD: at $f=0.001$ within $1\%$ (uniform $0.1306$ vs.\ $0.1321$; harmonic
$0.1113$ vs.\ $0.1120$); at $f=0.01$ within $2\%$ (uniform $0.0387$
vs.\ $0.0379$; harmonic $0.0285$ vs.\ $0.0280$). The plug-in is well
calibrated in this diagnostic.

\paragraph{Headline (Table~\ref{tab:are_full}).}
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})=4.53$.
\textbf{Uniform requires $\sim 4.5\times$ the impressions of harmonic for
matching precision}, with $\mathrm{SE}_U/\mathrm{SE}_H\approx 2.13$. The
min heuristic~\cite{agarwal2019estimating} achieves $4.41$, the count-only
oracle $4.57$; harmonic is the principled parameter-free optimum.

\paragraph{Stratified ARE (Table~\ref{tab:strat}).}
Schemes tie on noise singletons (where there is nothing to estimate) and
harmonic dominates uniform by $5\times$ on the high-information stratum.
The gain comes from \emph{not} treating singletons as equal to
million-impression cells.

\paragraph{Weight-cap robustness.}
The harmonic ESS of $1{,}849$ out of $4.61$M pairs invites a concern that
the efficiency gain rests on a few extreme cells, but the small ESS
reflects deliberate concentration on the pairs containing nearly all
statistical information in this heavy-tailed setting rather than
instability of the estimator. Capping $\omega_i^h$ at
the $99.99$th percentile (threshold $2{,}079$; $462$ pairs capped) lifts
ESS to $5.2{\times}10^4$ and still gives
$\mathrm{ARE}=4.12$; at the $99$th percentile, $\mathrm{ARE}=2.83$ (ESS
$7.6{\times}10^5$); even at the $90$th, $\mathrm{ARE}=1.86$. Harmonic
beats uniform robustly across capping levels.

\paragraph{Estimand under PBM mis-specification.}
\label{par:estimand}
PBM is a known approximation; on real logs different weight schemes
estimate different functionals of cell-specific ratios. Specifically, the
population limit of $\hat R$ under any weight $\omega$ is
\[
R_\omega \;=\; \frac{\sum_i \omega_i\, \theta_k^i}{\sum_i \omega_i\, \theta_{k'}^i},
\]
which equals $p_k/p_{k'}$ only when $\theta_k^i/\theta_{k'}^i$ is the same
constant across cells---i.e.\ when PBM holds exactly. Under mis-specification
each scheme thus targets a different weighted average of the cell-specific
ratios $\rho_i=\theta_k^i/\theta_{k'}^i$ (uniform $\to 1.68$, harmonic
$\to 1.71$, $\theta$-aware oracle $\to 2.34$ on KDD). The plug-in
$\widetilde V$ comparison is a precision statement at fixed
$\omega$---a model-based efficiency comparison---and does \emph{not} claim
that one $R_\omega$ is the ``correct'' position-bias ratio. Choosing
$\omega$ thus involves a precision/estimand trade-off; harmonic's
attractive property is that, among count-only schemes, it concentrates on
high-information cells (where $\rho_i$ is estimated most precisely)
without depending on noisy plug-in $\hat\theta_i$.

\paragraph{Quantifying PBM mis-specification (Cochran's $Q$).}
\label{sec:cochran}
PBM is a known approximation, not a literal model -- prior
work~\cite{yue2010beyond,wang2018position,chuklin2015clickmodels} has long
established that real logs violate it. The relevant question is how
\emph{large} the deviation is. Cochran's $Q$ on the $16{,}132$
high-information pairs ($\ge 5$ clicks at each of positions $1$ and $2$)
gives $Q=6.17{\times}10^4$, $\mathrm{df}=16{,}131$, $Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$:
per-cell odds-ratio variability is $\sim 3.8\times$ what binomial sampling
alone would produce, well outside Poisson dispersion. This magnitude is
the quantitative driver of the estimand drift discussed in
Paragraph~\ref{par:estimand}; plug-in $\widetilde V$ keeps the efficiency
comparison well-posed by working at fixed $\omega$.

\section{Synthetic and Yahoo-LTR Validation}
\label{sec:synth}

To validate the ARE prediction in settings with ground truth, we run two
controlled experiments.

\paragraph{Anchor-item imbalance sweep (synthetic).}
Following~\cite{agarwal2019estimating}, $M{=}10$ positions, $p_k=k^{-1}$,
anchor-item model with traffic split $s$ and imbalance ratio $r$, 500 trials.
At $s=0.80$, $r=100$ harmonic reduces variance by $43.6\%$ vs.\ uniform,
$5\%$ vs.\ min; reduction grows to $\sim 54\%$ at $s=0.90,r=50$.
Across the $(s,r)$ grid, the data-equivalence factor
$\mathrm{DEF}=b_{\mathrm{uni}}/b_{\mathrm{harm}}$ has median $1.31$ and
maximum $1.94$.

\paragraph{Yahoo-LTR semi-synthetic.}
Same simulator but with real graded relevance from Yahoo Learning to Rank
Challenge Set~1 (6936 test queries; clicks simulated via PBM). Harmonic
reduces variance by $\sim 40\%$ vs.\ uniform at imbalance $r=50$
(Var $0.00015 \to 0.00009$; ${\sim}458\times$ lower MSE than aggressive
clipping). Production-style traffic simulation with $K{=}5$ concurrent
rankers (one default at $80\%$ + four treatment at $5\%$ each, swap
probability $0.5$): under uniform traffic only $0.4\%$ of pairs exceed
ratio~$10$, but under production traffic $16.7\%$ do; harmonic's MSE
reduction grows from $6.3\%$ to $\textbf{17.2\%}$, confirming the gain
scales with traffic-induced imbalance.

\section{Conclusion}

Framing harmonic weighting as the count-only inverse-variance optimum
(Theorems~\ref{thm:ivw}--\ref{thm:asym}) and replacing bootstrap with the
plug-in delta-method variance $\widetilde V$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:Vtilde}) yields
rigorous, calibration-verified efficiency comparisons on heavy-tailed real
click logs. On the full KDD Cup 2012 Track 2 log,
$\mathrm{ARE}(\text{harmonic},\text{uniform})=4.53$, rising to $\sim 5\times$
on information-bearing cells, with PBM mis-specification quantified separately
by Cochran-$Q$ ($Q/\mathrm{df}=3.83$). Future work: shrinkage variants of the
$\theta$-aware oracle (Theorem~\ref{thm:asym} with $\hat\theta_i$
regularised); click models beyond
PBM~\cite{wang2018position,yue2010beyond,chuklin2015clickmodels} where the
intervention-harvesting family must be reformulated.
